Q1: What is BERT and how does it work?
Answer: BERT is a multi-layer bidirectional Transformer encoder, as described in the BERT paper (Source: BERT_Paper.pdf, Page: 2). It works by first being pre-trained on unlabeled data over different pre-training tasks, and then fine-tuned using labeled data from downstream tasks (Source: BERT_Paper.pdf, Page: 2). The pre-training tasks include Masked LM and Next Sentence Prediction (NSP) (Source: BERT_Paper.pdf, Page: 3). During pre-training, the model is trained to predict masked tokens and to predict whether two sentences are adjacent in the original text (Source: BERT_Paper.pdf, Page: 3). The model uses a unified architecture across different tasks, with minimal difference between the pre-trained architecture and the final downstream architecture (Source: BERT_Paper.pdf, Page: 2). 

The input representation is constructed by summing the corresponding token, segment, and position embeddings (Source: BERT_Paper.pdf, Page: 3). The model also uses special tokens such as [CLS] and [SEP] to differentiate between sentences and to indicate the start and end of a sequence (Source: BERT_Paper.pdf, Page: 3). 

Overall, BERT's architecture and pre-training tasks allow it to capture a wide range of linguistic and semantic information, making it a powerful tool for a variety of natural language processing tasks.

Q2: Explain the self-attention mechanism in Transformers
Answer: The self-attention mechanism in Transformers is explained as follows: In the encoder, self-attention layers allow each position to attend to all positions in the previous layer of the encoder (Source: Attention_Is_All_You_Need.pdf, Page: 4). Similarly, in the decoder, self-attention layers allow each position to attend to all positions in the decoder up to and including that position (Source: Attention_Is_All_You_Need.pdf, Page: 4). However, to prevent leftward information flow in the decoder, the self-attention sub-layer in the decoder stack is modified to prevent positions from attending to subsequent positions (Source: Attention_Is_All_You_Need.pdf, Page: 2). 

In more detail, the self-attention mechanism is a sub-layer in each layer of the encoder and decoder, where the keys, values, and queries come from the same place, which is the output of the previous layer (Source: Attention_Is_All_You_Need.pdf, Page: 4). This allows the model to attend to all positions in the input sequence and weigh their importance. 

The self-attention mechanism is also combined with residual connections and layer normalization to facilitate the flow of information and prevent vanishing gradients (Source: Attention_Is_All_You_Need.pdf, Page: 2).

Q4: How does LoRA improve fine-tuning efficiency?
Answer: LoRA improves fine-tuning efficiency by not requiring the accumulated gradient update to weight matrices to have full-rank during adaptation, and by allowing the training of a subset of the pre-trained parameters (LoRA_Paper.pdf, Page 3). Additionally, LoRA reduces the need to retune hyperparameters when varying the rank 'r' due to a scaling that helps (LoRA_Paper.pdf, Page 3, citing Yang & Hu, 2021). This enables LoRA to roughly recover the expressiveness of full fine-tuning while reducing the number of trainable parameters.

Q10: RAG Architecture ?
